# Cortex Pilot Acquisition Strategy — Zero-Network Launch

> *Prepared by founding CTO, 2026-07-15*
> *This replaces the GTM assumptions in scaling-strategy.md, roadmap.md, and monetization.md.*
> *The constraint: founder has zero warm connections in any candidate vertical. All pilots must be acquired from a standing start.*

---

## 1. Honest Assessment

From zero network, acquiring 3 highly engaged healthcare practice owners while solo-building the product is the single hardest constraint in the v1 plan. The founder review flagged this as risks BR2 and BR3. Let's be direct about what each channel can and cannot do.

| Channel | Trust Level | Volume Potential | Speed to Pilot | Founder Time Cost | Realistic Pilot Yield (6 months) |
|---------|-------------|-----------------|----------------|-------------------|----------------------------------|
| Cold email | Low | High (100s/week) | 8–12 weeks | Medium (research + writing) | 0–1 |
| Cold LinkedIn | Low-Medium | Medium (20-30/week) | 8–12 weeks | Medium | 0–1 |
| Walking into businesses | Low | Low (5-10/day) | 4–6 weeks | High (travel time) | 0–1 |
| Local business groups | High | Low (5-15 contacts/month) | 6–10 weeks | High (event attendance) | 1–2 |
| Industry conferences | High | Medium (15-30 contacts/event) | 4–8 weeks | Medium (event + follow-up) | 1–2 |
| Industry study clubs / meetups | High | Low (5-10 contacts/month) | 4–8 weeks | Medium | 1–2 |
| Partnerships (PMS vendors, consultants) | High (borrowed trust) | Medium | 12–16 weeks | Medium (relationship building) | 0–2 |
| Founder personal network (2nd degree) | Medium-High | Low | 2–4 weeks | Low | 0–1 |
| Local business associations | Medium-High | Medium | 6–10 weeks | Medium (event attendance) | 1–2 |

**Key insight:** No single channel gets to 3 pilots fast enough. The strategy must be multi-channel and sequenced. And critically: the founder must start networking in week 1, not wait until the product is built.

---

## 2. The Strategy: "Build in Public + Learn in Person"

The core idea: the founder does not sell Cortex to strangers. The founder *studies* practice owners' operational problems publicly, builds relationships through genuine curiosity, and converts those relationships into pilots when the product is ready.

### Why this works (and cold outreach doesn't):

- Healthcare practice owners are pitched constantly — billing services, equipment, software, insurance, marketing. They have pitch-deafness.
- They never get: "I'm building something for practice owners and I want to understand your operations before I write a line of code. Can I shadow your office manager for two hours?"
- The ask is asymmetrical: you're asking for *information*, not money. Most owners will say yes to a curious builder who shows genuine interest in their business. Very few will say yes to "try my unproven software."
- After two hours shadowing their operations, you're not a stranger anymore. You're someone who understands their specific pain. When the product exists, the conversion conversation is: "Remember those scheduling gaps we talked about? I built something for that."

## 3. Sequenced Execution Plan

### Phase 0: Foundation (Weeks 1–4) — BUILD NETWORK, NOT PRODUCT FIRST

*This runs in parallel with the Phase 0 build from the roadmap. The founder splits time: mornings = code, afternoons = people.*

| Week | Activity | Goal | Time Investment |
|------|----------|------|-----------------|
| 1 | Identify 3 local healthcare practice study clubs / meetup groups. Join. Identify next dental/PT/chiropractic conference within driving distance. Join local Chamber of Commerce. | Have 3-4 events on the calendar for weeks 2-4 | 4 hours |
| 2 | Attend first event. Don't pitch. Ask: "What's the hardest operational problem in your practice?" Take notes. Follow up with 3 people who gave interesting answers — ask for a 30-minute coffee. | 3 coffee meetings scheduled | 6 hours (event + follow-up) |
| 3 | Coffee meetings. Shadow one office manager for 2 hours. See their PMS, their scheduling workflow, their no-show follow-up process. Ask: "If you had a magic button that solved one operational problem, what would it be?" | Deep understanding of real pain. 5-8 practice owners in network. | 8 hours |
| 4 | Second event. Same pattern. Now have 10-15 contacts. Identify the 3-4 who are most frustrated with their current tools and most excited about what you're building. | 3-4 warm leads for pilots | 6 hours |

**Phase 0 outcome:** 10–15 practice owners who know the founder by name. 3–4 who've expressed genuine interest in Cortex. Zero sales conversations — only learning.

### Phase 1: Warm the Leads (Weeks 5–8) — PRODUCT EXISTS, SHOW DON'T TELL

*The MVP should be functional by week 8 (Stripe + QuickBooks + Calendly connected, first decision cards). During weeks 5-8, keep the leads warm while building.*

| Week | Activity | Goal | Time Investment |
|------|----------|------|-----------------|
| 5 | Send a personal update to the 3-4 warm leads: "Here's what I learned from our conversation. I'm building the scheduling gap detector we talked about. Can I show you a preview in 3 weeks?" | Keep engagement alive. Set expectation. | 2 hours |
| 6 | Attend a third event if available. Otherwise: write a LinkedIn post about something you learned from shadowing practice owners (anonymized). Tag nobody. Just demonstrate credibility. | 5-10 new LinkedIn connections in the vertical. | 3 hours |
| 7 | Reach out to 2-3 practice management consultants or dental/PT coaches. "I'm building a tool that helps practice owners reduce no-shows. I'd love your perspective — 20 minutes?" These are future referral partners. | 2-3 coaches/consultants in network. | 3 hours |
| 8 | Product demo ready. Send to the 3-4 warm leads: "Here's the scheduling gap detector we discussed. It's rough but it works. Want to try it for free? I'll set it up personally." | First pilot commitment. | 4 hours |

**Phase 1 outcome:** 1–2 pilots committed. Real product in their hands. Founder has direct line to their feedback.

### Phase 2: Expand (Weeks 9–12) — REFERRALS + TARGETED OUTREACH

*With 1-2 pilots live, the acquisition flywheel starts. Pilots refer peers. The product itself becomes the sales tool.*

| Week | Activity | Goal | Time Investment |
|------|----------|------|-----------------|
| 9 | Pilot #1 onboarding. White-glove setup. Sit with them as they connect their tools. Watch their face when the first decision card appears. | Pilot #1 live, first feedback. | 8 hours |
| 10 | Ask Pilot #1: "Who else do you know who runs multiple practices and would benefit from this?" Get 1-2 introductions. Also: targeted LinkedIn outreach to 10 practice owners in metro area with specific, personalized message referencing a real problem. | 2-3 new leads. | 6 hours |
| 11 | Pilot #2 onboarded (from referral or LinkedIn). Pilot #1 has been live for 2 weeks — collect their first ROI story: "Cortex found a $4,200 booking gap next Thursday." This becomes your sales collateral. | Pilot #2 live. First ROI story. | 8 hours |
| 12 | Pilot #3 onboarded. If referrals aren't producing, use the ROI story from Pilot #1 in targeted cold outreach: "We helped a [city] dental practice recover $4,200 in one week. Want to see how?" | Pilot #3 live. | 6 hours |

**Phase 2 outcome:** 3 pilots live. At least 1 ROI story to share. Founder has 15-25 practice owners in network. Referral engine beginning.

### Phase 3: Stabilize (Weeks 13–16) — ITERATE + BACKFILL

*If any pilot churns, replace them. If all 3 stay, focus entirely on product improvement from their feedback.*

| Week | Activity | Goal |
|------|----------|------|
| 13-14 | Weekly check-in calls with all 3 pilots. Fix their top 3 complaints within 48 hours. | Pilots feel heard. Product improves. |
| 15 | Conversion conversation with Pilot #1 (2-month free period ending). "Here's what Cortex found. Here's the ROI. Continue at $500/mo?" | First paid conversion (or honest churn feedback). |
| 16 | Conversion conversations with Pilots #2 and #3. If any churn, recruit replacement from existing warm leads or ask remaining pilots for referrals. | 3 paying customers (or clear data on why not). |

## 4. Channel Comparison & Integration

The strategy above integrates multiple channels. Here's how they work together:

### Primary Channels (70% of effort)

**1. Local, in-person networking (study clubs, associations, conferences)**
- **Why primary:** Highest trust per hour invested. Practice owners who meet you in person at a study club are 10× more likely to become pilots than anyone you reach cold.
- **Tactics:** Don't sell. Learn. Ask about their operations. Shadow their staff. Become known as "the guy building that scheduling thing."
- **Target:** 1-2 pilots from this channel.
- **Risk:** Slow to scale. Geographic limitation (must be within driving distance). Mitigation: target multi-location practices where one owner relationship = access to multiple locations.

**2. Pilot referrals**
- **Why primary:** Once Pilot #1 is live and seeing value, their referral to a peer is the highest-converting channel possible. "My friend uses this and it saved him $4K" is unbeatable.
- **Tactics:** Ask explicitly. "Who else should be using this?" Make the referral easy: "Can I mention your name when I reach out to Dr. Smith?"
- **Target:** 1-2 pilots from this channel.
- **Risk:** Depends on Pilot #1 being happy. If Pilot #1 has a bad experience, referrals die. Mitigation: over-invest in Pilot #1's success.

### Secondary Channels (30% of effort)

**3. LinkedIn (targeted, not mass)**
- **Why secondary:** Response rates are low, but the cost is also low. Use sparingly to supplement primary channels.
- **Tactics:** Don't send InMail pitches. Post content about what you're learning from practice owners. Engage with their posts. Build presence before asking.
- **Target:** 0-1 pilots from this channel. More useful for building general awareness than direct conversion.

**4. Practice management consultants / coaches**
- **Why secondary:** These are force multipliers — one consultant serves 20+ practices. But building these relationships takes 12+ weeks, pushing beyond the 6-month window.
- **Tactics:** Start relationships now, don't expect pilots from them. Plant seeds for v2 growth.
- **Target:** 0 pilots in 6 months. 2-3 referral partners by month 12.

### Channels NOT Recommended for v1

**5. Cold email**
- **Why not:** Healthcare practice owners receive 20+ cold pitches per week. Your email about "autonomous strategic decision support" dies in the same inbox as "SEO services for dentists." Without a warm introduction or a specific, named referral, cold email conversion is near-zero. The time is better spent at a local study club.

**6. Walking into businesses**
- **Why not:** Healthcare practices are not walk-in businesses. The owner is either with patients or at another location. You'll talk to a receptionist who will politely take your card and throw it away. This works for retail and restaurants, not healthcare.

**7. Paid acquisition (Google Ads, Meta)**
- **Why not:** Too early. You don't know your message, your conversion funnel, or your CAC. Paid ads without product-market fit just burn cash. Revisit at 30+ customers.

## 5. Target Pilot Profile — Updated

Given zero-network acquisition, the pilot profile needs to be more specific to make outreach efficient:

| Criterion | Requirement | Why |
|-----------|-------------|-----|
| **Vertical** | Multi-location healthcare (dental, PT, chiropractic, optometry) | Per vertical analysis |
| **Locations** | 2–5 locations | Multi-location = more data, more pain, higher willingness to pay |
| **Revenue** | $1M–5M | Sweet spot for Essentials tier. Big enough to have real problems, small enough that owner is accessible |
| **Geography** | Within 60-minute drive of founder | Face-to-face onboarding is non-negotiable for pilots. Remote onboarding is for v2 customers. |
| **Tool stack** | At least Stripe + QuickBooks + a modern PMS (Jane, Mindbody, Dentrix, WebPT) | Minimum integration set. If they don't have these, onboarding fails. |
| **Owner personality** | Operationally frustrated. Has tried to fix things before. Wants data but doesn't have it. | Pilots who are "just curious" will churn. Pilots who are *in pain* will engage deeply. |
| **Decision-maker** | Owner or managing partner. Not an office manager who needs approval. | Sales cycles die when the person you're talking to isn't the buyer. |

## 6. The Founder's Weekly Split (Weeks 1–12)

This is the hardest part. The founder must code AND network simultaneously. Here's a realistic allocation:

| Activity | Hours/Week | Notes |
|----------|-----------|-------|
| Coding (product build) | 25–30 | Mornings. Deep work. No meetings. |
| Networking (events, coffee, shadowing) | 8–10 | Afternoons. 1-2 events/week + follow-ups. |
| Pilot communication (calls, emails, support) | 3–5 | Grows as pilots onboard. |
| Strategy, planning, writing | 3–5 | Evenings/weekends. |
| **Total** | **40–50** | Sustainable if protected. Burnout risk above 55. |

**The non-negotiable:** Two afternoons per week are reserved for in-person networking. If the product ships on time but nobody knows the founder, the company dies. The product and the network must be built simultaneously.

## 7. Risk Assessment

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|-----------|--------|------------|
| Founder can't split time between code and networking | High | Critical — no pilots | Ruthless time blocking. Mornings = code, afternoons = people. No exceptions. If coding falls behind, descope features, don't cancel networking. |
| Local healthcare community is small or insular | Medium | High — fewer events, harder to break in | Expand geographic radius to 90 minutes. Consider virtual study clubs. If local is truly dead, pivot to home services (more accessible in person). |
| Pilots recruited but product isn't ready | Medium | High — broken promise | Be honest about timeline. "I'm building this. It'll be ready in 8 weeks. Can I shadow you in the meantime?" Under-promise on delivery dates. |
| Pilots churn before conversion (month 2) | Medium | Medium | Over-invest in Pilot #1. Weekly calls. Fix bugs in 48 hours. Make them feel like co-builders, not customers. |
| 3 pilots acquired but all in same sub-vertical (e.g., all dental, no PT) | Low | Medium | Actively recruit across specialties. Dental for Pilot #1, PT for #2, chiropractic for #3. If they all end up dental, that's still useful — it just narrows the v2 expansion path. |

## 8. What Success Looks Like (Month 6)

- **Network:** 20–30 practice owners know the founder by name. 5–10 have had substantive conversations about their operations.
- **Pilots:** 3 pilots active, using Cortex 3+ days/week.
- **Referrals:** At least 1 pilot has referred another practice owner (even if they didn't convert).
- **ROI story:** At least 1 pilot can point to a specific dollar amount Cortex helped them save or earn.
- **Conversion:** At least 1 pilot has converted to paid ($500/mo).
- **Learning:** Founder can articulate: "Here's exactly what multi-location healthcare practices need from Cortex, and here's what they don't care about."
